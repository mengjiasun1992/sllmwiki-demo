# Writing guide: skim read

## Audience and purpose

**Takeaway:** a reader from the field gets the work's context and main line of argument, understands its key part, and can decide whether to read it in full.

**Aim:** faithful, focused, decisive. Faithful: it says what the work says, in the work's terms, each claim cited to its section, with this primer's own judgment marked as such. Focused: the depth goes to the key part, at least 40% of the body, and everything else stays at primer level. Decisive: a reader knows who should read the full work, which parts, and why.

A reader from the same field, with its background, who hasn't decided whether to spend an hour or more on the work and finds its abstract too thin to decide on. In ten minutes they want what the work is about, how it makes its case, its key part understood well enough to use, and a verdict on reading further.

## Voice and tone

Plain and direct, present tense: "the paper argues", not "the authors attempted to argue". Report the work's claims as its claims; where the primer judges (how strong the evidence is, the verdict), say so and mark it.

## Structure

- **One work:** the narrative reads one source in full, the work itself. Other sources and narratives only place it, in Context, briefly; they never replace what the work says.
- **At a glance:** the facts a reader scans first, in a table.
- **Context:** for a reader who knows the field: name the background, don't teach it; spend the words on what the work responds to and adds.
- **The arc:** the work's own order, so a reader can follow the full text with it later; each part cited to its section.
- **The key part:** the one idea or result the work stands on, explained so the reader could restate it: its statement (quoted, briefly), why it holds, and what follows, on the work's own example. When the work has two co-equal results, explain both, more briefly; never spread the depth over everything.
- **How strong is it:** what kind of evidence backs the key part and how far it goes; open questions the work admits, then those it doesn't.
- **Should you read it:** a reading path by section, then a plain recommendation, for whom and why; no star rating.

## Examples

| Section | The work's example's part |
|---|---|
| Context | the situation it illustrates, before the work's idea |
| The key part | the idea applied to it, step by step |
| How strong is it | where it stops being representative |

## Evidence

The work itself is the source, and the narrative cites its sections throughout; its references are not read, only named when Context needs them. Quote briefly, within the wiki's quoting limit. Context may cite another saved source or link a narrative to place the work, never to say what the work says.

## Length

Ten minutes: the body within 2,000 words, the key part at least 40% of it. Cut background the reader has, and any section the work doesn't need, before cutting the key part.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every claim about the work cites its section; the primer's own judgment is marked.
- [ ] The key part is explained well enough to restate, and carries at least 40% of the body.
- [ ] The arc follows the work's own order, section by section.
- [ ] The verdict says who should read it in full, which parts, and why.
